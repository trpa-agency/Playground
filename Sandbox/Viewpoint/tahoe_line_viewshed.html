The line, or the polygon's perimeter, is sampled at the spacing you set, measured as true ground distance. Each sample becomes an observer. In whole-area polygon mode, every terrain cell whose center is inside the polygon also becomes an observer. At the grid's resolution, that matches sampling the inside every foot. For each observer, rays are cast across a terrain grid from the scene's ground elevation service. A cell is visible if its line of sight clears every terrain cell in between. The visible cells from all observers are merged with a logical OR on the grid, which is the same as a union of every per-point viewshed polygon. The merged grid is then traced into polygons.

Samples that fall in the same terrain cell (and, for direction of travel, face within 5° of each other) would give identical results at the grid's resolution, so each cell is computed only once.

The terrain is bare earth. With "Trees block the view" on, tree height from the 10 m Global Canopy Height 2020 layer (Lang et al. 2022, CC BY 4.0) is added on top of the terrain. Trees then block sight lines, and the tops of trees count as visible surface. Tree heights in that layer are typically off by several meters, and buildings are not included. With it off, the result is the largest area that could possibly be seen.
